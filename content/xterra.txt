### Low-level interface and controllers 

**Low-level motor API**. Essentially, I was developing a software layer of abstraction over the motor controller API that we were using. It involved setting the configurations correctly, the bootup sequence, while ensuring safe hardware limits. Developing the communication structure over ROS and ensuring stable rate. It is a very essential aspect of any robotics project of course and was fun to do. 

**Sensor Suite**. A part of my job was to develop the sensor suite for the whole robot. Processing the signal from the joint encoders to clean them. Obtaining data from the current sensors in the motor controller to get the torque was also a part of it. 

### Sim-2-real and actuator characterization

Reinforcement Learning was particularly promising for quadruped systems. I took that up as a challenge (was also kind of assigned to me). While the policy itself is a topic of another discussion, it was interesting to transfer it to the hardware where the actual challenges lay. We had to

- Ensure that the latency and noise was well characterized through distribution and we then replicated these distributions in the simulation
- Write up an actuator model in the simulation, and fill in the parameters to match the actuator behavior of the real robot. We ran a very interesting experiment where I hung the robot in the air and then ran a specified sequence of commands for each of the legs. Then we ran the same sequence in the simulation, and we found out a set of parameters that, if we implemented it in the actuator model in the simulation, it would mimic the real joint evolution fairly closely. This was actually a characterization as had been done by Hutter's group, and we kind of used it to train our model. We had to find $I_a$, $d$, saturation limits, $\tau_{\mathrm{comp}}$, $\tau_f$ and the PD gains. 

@math I_a\ddot{q} + d\dot{q} = \operatorname{sat}\!\left(P_{\tau}(\hat{q} - q + \tilde{q}_b) - D_{\tau}\dot{q} + \tau_{\mathrm{comp}}\right) + \tau_f

### Reinforcement Learning and the policy

With a clear path given to me by the rest of the team, I had to come up with an RL pipeline that would be powerful, easy to work with and extend. After a few months of learning RL, and researching the state of the art, we went ahead with IsaacLab which was still quite new at that time. We used the walk these ways paper as the base and got a policy that could produce different gaits.
